Limitations of Cross-Domain Transfer for Audio Deepfake Enhancement

Preprint · Currently under journal/conference review

Abstract: While prior work has focused on improving generation quality or developing detection systems, a separate question remains: can existing speech enhancement models remove synthetic artifacts from deepfakes without task-specific retraining? This paper investigates whether cross-domain transfer from speech enhancement to deepfake enhancement is feasible. We train a diffusion-based speech enhancement model (DR-DiffuSE) on the LJSpeech dataset and apply it to vocoder-generated speech from the WaveFake dataset without fine-tuning. Our experiments yield a clear negative result. Enhanced audio shows minimal improvement on traditional metrics and actually degrades perceptual quality compared to the original deepfakes, and this failure is consistent across every architecture and hyperparameter configuration we test, indicating a genuine boundary condition for transfer learning in audio rather than an isolated failed setting. The domain gap between acoustic noise (the training objective) and synthetic artifacts (the target application) is too large for naive transfer to succeed. However, we find that this failure is specific to perceptual naturalness: speaker-identity preservation, measured via automatic speaker verification, remains close to that of the unprocessed deepfakes even as perceptual quality degrades, suggesting that naive enhancement affects different detection signals differently. We establish baseline performance metrics for future research on cross-task transfer in audio processing, showing that while DiffuSEC with fast sampling achieves STOI 0.93, neither architecture produces natural-sounding speech. This negative finding contributes to understanding the limitations of transfer learning in audio domains and points to concrete next steps, including testing enhanced audio against a dedicated spoofing countermeasure and establishing a fine-tuned upper bound to isolate the cost of the domain gap.
